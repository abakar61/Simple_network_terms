# Metropolitan Area Network (MAN) — README

## 1. What Is a Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)?

A **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)** is a computer network that connects multiple networks across a **medium-sized geographical area**.

A MAN is generally:

**Larger than a LAN but smaller than a WAN.**

The term "metropolitan" refers mainly to the size and geographical coverage of the network. A MAN does not necessarily have to operate inside a city.

A MAN can connect networks across:

* A large city
* Several nearby towns
* Multiple buildings
* A large geographical area
* Multiple organizations located within the same region

---

## 2. MAN vs LAN vs WAN

The three network types can be distinguished mainly by geographical coverage.

### LAN — Local Area Network

A **LAN** covers a small area, such as:

* A home
* An office
* A school building
* A computer laboratory

### MAN — Metropolitan Area Network

A **MAN** covers a larger area than a LAN, such as:

* A city
* Multiple nearby buildings
* Several campuses
* A group of nearby organizations

### WAN — Wide Area Network

A **WAN** covers a very large geographical area, such as:

* Multiple cities
* Multiple countries
* Continents
* The entire world

The simple relationship is:

**LAN < MAN < WAN**

---

## 3. Purpose of a MAN

The main purpose of a MAN is to **connect multiple LANs within a relatively large geographical area**.

For example, an organization may have several buildings located across the same city. Instead of treating every building as an isolated network, a MAN can provide connectivity between their LANs.

MANs can also connect networks belonging to different organizations.

---

## 4. How MAN Networks Are Constructed

Like WANs, MANs are generally made up of **interconnected LANs**.

Because a MAN covers a smaller area than a WAN, the distance between connected networks is usually shorter. This can make communication more efficient and reduce latency compared with many long-distance WAN connections.

MANs can use different types of network infrastructure, including:

* Fiber-optic cables
* Ethernet
* Wireless links
* ISP infrastructure
* Metropolitan fiber networks

---

## 5. Fiber Optic in MANs

**Fiber-optic cable** is commonly used to build MAN infrastructure because it can provide:

* High bandwidth
* High data transmission speeds
* Long-distance communication
* Low signal loss
* Resistance to electromagnetic interference

Fiber is particularly suitable for connecting multiple buildings or locations across a city.

---

## 6. What Is Dark Fiber?

**Dark fiber** refers to fiber-optic cables that have been installed but are not currently being used to transmit data.

The fiber is called "dark" because it is not carrying active optical signals.

Organizations or service providers can lease dark fiber and use their own networking equipment to transmit data over it.

Using dark fiber can provide organizations with greater control over their network infrastructure, although it can require additional equipment and technical management.

---

## 7. Who Manages a MAN?

A MAN can be managed by different types of organizations.

For example, a MAN may be operated by:

* An Internet service provider
* A telecommunications company
* A city government
* A large organization
* A group of organizations
* A network infrastructure provider

Unlike a typical LAN, which is often managed by one organization, a MAN may provide connectivity to multiple organizations.

---

## 8. MAN and Service Providers

Many MANs rely on infrastructure provided by telecommunications companies or ISPs.

A service provider may build a metropolitan fiber network and provide connectivity to businesses, schools, government institutions, and other organizations.

Organizations can then use the provider's infrastructure without having to construct the entire physical network themselves.

---

## 9. MAN vs CAN

**CAN** stands for **Campus Area Network**.

A CAN connects multiple LANs across a relatively large campus, such as:

* A university campus
* A corporate campus
* A research campus
* A hospital campus

A CAN is usually smaller than a typical MAN.

However, there can be some overlap between the definitions of CAN and MAN because both can connect multiple LANs across several buildings.

A campus network may therefore sometimes be considered a small MAN.

---

## 10. MAN vs WAN

The main difference between a MAN and a WAN is their geographical scale.

### MAN

* Medium geographical coverage
* Usually covers a city or region
* Connects multiple LANs
* Often uses metropolitan fiber infrastructure
* Usually has shorter distances than WANs

### WAN

* Very large geographical coverage
* Can connect cities, countries, and continents
* Connects multiple LANs and MANs
* Can use long-distance telecommunications infrastructure
* Often depends on multiple service providers

---

## 11. Advantages of MAN

A MAN can provide several advantages:

* Connects multiple LANs
* Covers a larger area than a LAN
* Can provide high-speed connectivity
* Fiber infrastructure can provide high bandwidth
* Can connect multiple organizations
* Can support communication across a city or region
* Can provide centralized network services
* Can reduce the need for completely independent networks

---

## 12. Disadvantages of MAN

MANs can also have disadvantages:

* More expensive than LANs
* More complex to manage
* Requires specialized infrastructure
* May depend on telecommunications providers
* Requires strong security controls
* Physical cable failures can affect multiple organizations
* Expanding the network can require significant investment

---

## 13. Security of MAN Networks

Because MANs connect multiple networks and may serve many users or organizations, security is important.

A MAN can be exposed to various network threats, including:

* Unauthorized access
* Malware
* Denial-of-service attacks
* Distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks
* Data interception
* Network attacks
* Misconfiguration

Security mechanisms can include:

* Firewalls
* Encryption
* Authentication
* Access control
* Intrusion detection
* Intrusion prevention
* Traffic monitoring
* DDoS protection

---

## 14. DDoS Attacks

A **Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS)** attack attempts to overwhelm a network, server, or service with a large amount of traffic.

Large networks such as MANs can be targets because they may provide connectivity for many users and organizations.

DDoS protection can help detect and mitigate malicious traffic before it causes significant disruption.

---

## 15. Network Interconnection

A MAN can use dedicated interconnection services to connect networks to external network providers or cloud services.

A network interconnection can be provided through:

* Physical connections
* Virtual connections
* Private connections
* Internet-based connections

The goal is to provide efficient and secure communication between networks.

---

## 16. MAN Performance

Because a MAN generally covers a smaller geographical area than a WAN, communication can often have:

* Lower latency than long-distance WAN connections
* High bandwidth
* Faster communication between connected locations
* More predictable performance when dedicated fiber is used

Actual performance depends on the technology, distance, network equipment, provider, bandwidth, and network configuration.

---

## 17. Important MAN Terms

### MAN

**Metropolitan Area Network** — a network that connects multiple networks across a medium-sized geographical area.

### LAN

**Local Area Network** — a network covering a relatively small area.

### WAN

**Wide Area Network** — a network covering a large geographical area.

### CAN

**Campus Area Network** — a network connecting multiple buildings or LANs within a campus.

### Fiber Optic

A transmission medium that uses light to carry data through optical fiber.

### Dark Fiber

Installed fiber-optic cable that is not currently being used to transmit active network traffic.

### ISP

**Internet Service Provider** — a company that provides Internet or network connectivity.

### DDoS

**Distributed Denial-of-Service** — an attack that attempts to make a service or network unavailable by overwhelming it with traffic.

### Interconnection

The process of connecting separate networks so that they can communicate.

## 18. Key Characteristics of a MAN

A MAN generally has the following characteristics:

* Larger than a LAN
* Smaller than a WAN
* Connects multiple LANs
* Usually covers a city or region
* Can connect multiple buildings
* Can be operated by service providers or organizations
* Commonly uses fiber-optic infrastructure
* Can provide high-speed communication
* Requires appropriate security mechanisms

---

## 19. Summary

A **Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)** is a network that connects multiple LANs across a medium-sized geographical area.

A MAN is **larger than a LAN but smaller than a WAN**. It can cover a city, several nearby towns, multiple buildings, or another similarly sized geographical area.

MANs commonly use **fiber-optic infrastructure** because fiber provides high bandwidth, high speeds, long-distance transmission, and low signal loss. Some MANs use **dark fiber**, which refers to unused fiber-optic infrastructure that can be leased and activated by organizations or service providers.

MANs can be operated by telecommunications companies, ISPs, governments, organizations, or groups of organizations. They can also connect multiple organizations within the same geographical region.

The simplest way to remember the network types is:

**LAN → small area**

**MAN → city or medium-sized area**

**WAN → large geographical area**

Therefore:

**LAN < MAN < WAN**

# Transmission Media in Computer Networks

## 📌 Introduction

Transmission media is the pathway used to carry data signals from one device to another in a computer network. It plays an important role in determining network speed, reliability, security, and performance.

There are two main types of transmission media:

1. **Guided Media (Wired)**
   - Uses physical cables to transmit data.
   - Examples: Twisted Pair Cable, Coaxial Cable, Fiber Optic Cable.

2. **Unguided Media (Wireless)**
   - Uses electromagnetic waves to transmit data through air or space.
   - Examples: Radio Waves, Microwaves, Infrared, Satellite Communication.

---

# Characteristics of Transmission Media

## 1. Bandwidth

Bandwidth is the maximum amount of data that a transmission medium can carry in a given time.

- Measured in bits per second (bps).
- Fiber optic cables provide higher bandwidth than copper cables.

## 2. Delay and Latency

Latency is the time required for data to travel from the sender to the receiver.

Lower latency means faster communication.

## 3. Cost

The cost includes:

- Installation cost.
- Maintenance cost.
- Operational expenses.

Example:
Fiber optic cables are expensive to install but have lower maintenance costs.

## 4. Attenuation

Attenuation is the reduction of signal strength as data travels through a medium.

Solutions:

- Use repeaters.
- Use amplifiers.
- Reduce transmission distance.

## 5. Security and EMI Protection

Some media are more secure than others.

- Fiber optic cables are resistant to electromagnetic interference (EMI).
- Wireless communication is easier to intercept.

---

# Types of Transmission Media

## 1. Guided Media (Wired)

Guided media uses a physical path to carry signals.

## A) Twisted Pair Cable

Twisted pair cable consists of two copper wires twisted together to reduce interference.

Types:

### UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair)

- No protective shield.
- Commonly used in Ethernet networks.

### STP (Shielded Twisted Pair)

- Contains shielding to reduce interference.
- Used in environments with high noise.

### Advantages

- Cheap.
- Easy to install.
- Supports Ethernet networks.

### Disadvantages

- Limited distance.
- More affected by interference than fiber.

---

# B) Coaxial Cable

Coaxial cable contains:

- Central copper conductor.
- Insulation layer.
- Metallic shield.
- Outer protective cover.

Used in:

- Cable TV.
- Broadband internet.

### Advantages

- Good protection against interference.
- High bandwidth.

### Disadvantages

- Difficult installation.
- More expensive than twisted pair.

---

# C) Fiber Optic Cable

Fiber optic cable transmits data using light signals through glass or plastic fibers.

Used for:

- Internet backbone.
- Long-distance communication.
- High-speed networks.

### Advantages

- Extremely high bandwidth.
- Very low signal loss.
- Immune to electromagnetic interference.

### Disadvantages

- Expensive.
- Fragile.
- Difficult to repair.

---

# 2. Unguided Media (Wireless)

Unguided media transmits electromagnetic waves through air or space.

---

# A) Radio Waves

Radio waves are wireless signals that can travel long distances and pass through walls.

Used in:

- Radio broadcasting.
- Mobile communication.
- Wireless networks.

### Advantages

- Wide coverage.
- Can penetrate obstacles.

### Disadvantages

- More interference.
- Less secure.

---

# B) Microwaves

Microwave communication uses high-frequency radio waves.

It requires:

- Line of Sight (LoS).
- Proper antenna alignment.

Used in:

- Satellite communication.
- Mobile networks.
- Point-to-point links.

### Advantages

- High-speed communication.
- Long-distance transmission.

### Disadvantages

- Affected by weather.
- Requires clear line of sight.

---

# C) Infrared

Infrared uses light waves for short-range communication.

Used in:

- Remote controls.
- Short-range devices.

### Advantages

- Secure.
- Resistant to radio interference.

### Disadvantages

- Short range.
- Cannot pass through walls.

---

# Satellite Communication

Satellite communication is a type of microwave transmission that uses satellites to transfer signals around the world.

### Advantages

- Global coverage.
- Works in remote areas.

### Disadvantages

- Expensive.
- High latency.
- Weather can affect signals.

---

# Transmission Impairments

Transmission impairments reduce the quality of signals during communication.

## 1. Attenuation

Loss of signal strength.

**Solution:**
- Amplifiers.
- Repeaters.

## 2. Distortion

The signal shape changes during transmission.

**Solution:**
- Signal correction techniques.

## 3. Noise

Unwanted signals interfere with communication.

**Solution:**
- Shielding.
- Filtering.
- Proper grounding.

Important factors:

| Factor | Description |
|---|---|

| Distance | Longer distances require better media |
| Interference | Some media resist noise better |

Transmission media is the foundation of computer networks. It allows data to move between devices.

The two main categories are:

### Guided Media
- Twisted Pair Cable
- Coaxial Cable
- Fiber Optic Cable

### Unguided Media
- Radio Waves
- Microwaves
- Infrared
